Interpretation and limits

The low test-split ratio reflects the tokenizer’s fit to a large Urdu-script corpus, while Roman Urdu is the clearest weakness in the selected set. Digit normalization is applied when preparing the corpus, but the hand-written diagnostic retains mixed digit forms, so its result also tests normalization mismatch.

The three failure cases are maxima from seven manually selected inputs, not a representative benchmark. Tokens-per-word is a compression measure only; it does not evaluate semantic quality, downstream model performance, or fairness across Urdu varieties.

Urdu-aware pre-tokenization

's|'t|'re|'ve|'m|'ll|'d| ?[\p{L}\p{M}\u200c]+| ?\p{N}+| ?[^\s\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}\u200c]+|\s+(?!\S)|\s+
AlternativeBehavior and reason
's|'t|'re|'ve|'m|'ll|'dKeep common English contractions as separate pieces for naturally code-mixed Urdu.
?[\p{L}\p{M}\u200c]+Group Unicode letters, combining marks, and ZWNJ. Marks and joiners stay inside the pre-token, so they do not create artificial BPE boundaries. \p{L} and \p{M} cover Urdu and other scripts.
?\p{N}+Group a digit run independently from words; the optional leading ASCII space remains attached to it.
?[^\s\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}\u200c]+Group runs of punctuation or symbols, excluding whitespace, letters, marks, digits, and ZWNJ; this avoids splitting punctuation into single-character pieces.
\s+(?!\S)|\s+Consume whitespace runs, preferring trailing whitespace as one piece and then matching any remaining run. Together, the alternatives cover newlines without losing them.

Because BPE counts pairs within each pre-token, no learned merge crosses these boundaries. The normalization step is separate; this pattern preserves ZWNJ rather than removing it.
